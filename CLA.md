# Ferrite Contributor Agreement

Version 1, November 21, 2026. It covers the FerriteFW organization's public repos that link to it: Ferrite, Ferrite Console, the manual and the website (the project).

Thank you for contributing. A contribution is anything you send to be included in the project: code, documents, images, data. When you agree, this applies to all your contributions:

1. **You keep your copyright.** You give Alex Harvey, the project's maintainer, and anyone he passes the project to, a free, worldwide, permanent, irrevocable licence to use, copy, change, publish, sublicense and license your contribution on any terms, commercial ones too. This includes a licence under any patents of yours that your contribution uses. So the project can be licensed to a radio maker without coming back to ask you. That never replaces the public version (point 2).
2. **It stays public.** Every published version of the project that includes your contribution stays licensed to everyone under that repo's licence (the PolyForm Noncommercial License 1.0.0; for the manual, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), or a licence that gives everyone at least the same rights. That licence is never withdrawn.
3. **If the project stops.** When either of these happens, you license your contribution to everyone under the MIT licence from that day, and Alex does the same for his own code (`STEWARDS.md`):
   - The project is archived: by Alex, or, if he can't be reached for six months, by the stewards in `STEWARDS.md`. Archived means the public repos are marked archived and their READMEs say the project has stopped. Can't be reached means no answer by email or on GitHub for six months. An archive can't be undone.
   - Seven years pass after the project's latest release with no newer one. A release is a version tagged and published in the public repo of Ferrite or Ferrite Console.
4. **It's yours to give.** You wrote it, or have the right to send it, and you've marked anything that came from someone else. If your employer owns what you write, it agrees to this agreement for that work, and you have the authority to agree for it. Don't send anything from stock radio firmware, the SI4732 SSB patch, other people's recordings or flash dumps, or code under a licence that doesn't allow this agreement (such as the GPL).
5. **Changes and credit.** You won't use your moral rights to object to changes to your contribution or to how the project is used or licensed. You'll still be credited in the project's history and credits.
6. **No promises either way.** The project doesn't have to use your contribution, and you give it as it is, without warranty, apart from point 4.
7. **The law.** The laws of British Columbia and of Canada apply, and its courts.
8. **If this changes.** A new version of this agreement applies only to contributions you send after you agree to it.

**How you agree.** Tick the box in the pull request template on your first pull request. For contributions made before this agreement, agree in a comment on the agreement's tracking issue. Your name (as you want it shown), GitHub name and the date go in [CLA-SIGNED.md](CLA-SIGNED.md), which is public. It covers all your contributions, past and future. A pull request isn't merged until its author has agreed. If you're under 19, a parent or guardian agrees too.
