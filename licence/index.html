Licence

Ferrite is free, and its source is public. It's licensed under the PolyForm Noncommercial License 1.0.0. Copyright 2026 Alex Harvey.

What you can do

Use, study, change and share Ferrite for any noncommercial purpose. The licence names hobby projects and amateur pursuits, and noncommercial organizations such as charities, schools, public safety and government. Hams, clubs and emergency groups are covered.

What needs permission

Selling Ferrite, shipping it on radios for sale, or selling a service built on it needs a separate commercial licence from the copyright holder. Radio manufacturers: see working with us.

Why this licence

AI tools now make forking and repackaging firmware nearly effortless. We want the work, the fixes and the people to stay in one place, where everyone benefits, rather than split across many copies. And nobody should be able to take Ferrite and sell it.

You can still change Ferrite and share your changes for noncommercial use. We'd rather those changes come back to the project, as bug reports, ideas or contributions on GitHub, so everyone's radio gets them.

Because it rules out commercial use, it isn't "open source" in the OSI's sense. We say free, with its source public.

Contributions

You keep the copyright in what you contribute. You let the project use and license it, commercial licences included, so nobody has to track you down later. A commercial licence never replaces the public one: every published version stays public under this licence or a more open one. If Ferrite is ever archived, or goes seven years without a release, it can move to MIT, so it stays useful to everyone.

The agreement is short; you agree to it on your first pull request.
