For the Radtel RT-950 Pro, one task at a time.

Chapter 1

Getting started

Ferrite is replacement firmware for the Radtel RT-950 Pro, and radios built on the same hardware. It is free, and its source is public. This guide shows you how to do things on the radio, one task at a time.

How keys are written:

  • MENU053 means press MENU, then 0, then 5, then 3.
  • holdABC means hold the ABC key for about a second.
  • knob+1 means turn the knob one click to the right. knob−1 is one click to the left.
  • SIDE1+knob means hold SIDE1 and turn the knob.
  • ▲▼ and ◀▶ are the arrow keys.

Install Ferrite

Why Ferrite goes on the radio through the radio's own bootloader. You can always put a new image on, or go back to the original firmware.

Before you start:

  • You need the programming cable and a computer with Chrome, Edge or Firefox, or the Ferrite Console desktop app.
  • Keep the stock firmware file (the .BTF). You need it to go back, and the HF receiver's SSB mode comes from it.
  • Installing replaces the radio's channels and settings. Console won't install until it has a full backup of the radio and you have saved it. Keep that file safe, off the computer too.

In short, on Ferrite Console, Firmware › Install:

  1. Plug in the cable and turn the radio on. Any firmware is fine.
  2. Connect, then Back up the radio (about 4 minutes) and save the backup file.
  3. SSB (optional): drop in your stock .BTF.
  4. Install Ferrite: about a minute. The radio restarts, Ferrite sets up its storage and opens setup.

Chapter 10 has each step, what to do if the install stops, and how to go back to the stock firmware.

Your calibration is never erased. Nothing comes across from the old firmware, so you add channels again after installing (or import them in Console).

First run: setup

Why setup asks the few things the radio needs to know. Every page has a sensible default.

  1. Turn the radio on. Setup starts by itself on a new install.
  2. Answer each page, then press OK for the next one. BACK goes to the previous page. # skips the rest (on the callsign and owner card pages, # changes the letters instead).
  3. The pages are:
    • Callsign. Type it on the keypad: the digits type letters, and # switches between letters and digits. You can leave it empty, but APRS and some transmit features need it.
    • Region. Pick where you are. It sets where you may transmit and a few regional defaults.
    • Owner card. Your name and contact. Optional. * types a space, # changes case, and 1 has . - / @ for an e-mail address.
    • Time. Time zone, clock format and units. The GPS sets the clock once it has a fix.
    • Look. The home layout and the colour theme.
    • Battery Save. How much the receivers sleep to save battery.
    • Summary. Your choices.
  4. After setup, the radio is ready to receive and transmit. It starts with no channels.

To run setup again: MENU0971.

The home screen and the three slots

Why everything starts from home. Knowing the slots makes the rest easy.

  • The radio has three slots: A, B and C. Each one is on a VFO (any frequency) or a channel.
  • The active slot is the big one. Your keys and the knob work on it.
  • The other two are shown as cards. The radio keeps listening to them too. This is called watch.
  • Press ABC to make the next slot active: A, then B, then C.
  • PTT A (the big PTT) transmits on the active slot. PTT B (the smaller one) always transmits on slot B.
  • The bar at the top shows the clock, the screen's name, and marks for the key lock, scan (SCN), watch (DW for two slots, TW for three), unread messages, GPS, Bluetooth, Low Power Mode (LOW) and the battery.
  • The bar at the bottom shows what the main keys do on this screen.

To go home from anywhere: holdBACK.

Glance at the status

Why one page with everything the status bar can't fit.

  1. Open MENU00 (hold MENU, then 0).
  2. Read transmit state, region, inbox, scan, watch, beacon, Battery Save, GPS, Bluetooth, battery and the key lock. OK on a row opens its setting.
  3. Press BACK to return.

If a watched slot is receiving, # pauses watch instead. The bottom bar says "# Pause" when that is what it will do.

The keys

Why Ferrite has one fixed key layout. Learn it once and it never changes.

  • MENU/OK: on home, opens quick settings. Held, opens the full menu.
  • BACK: back one step. Held, goes home from anywhere.
  • Knob and ▲▼: tune, or step through channels. In lists, the knob changes the highlighted value and ▲▼ move.
  • ◀▶: on a channel, change zone. On a VFO, change band.
  • ABC: next slot. Held, edit the slot.
  • V/M: switch between VFO and channels. Held, open the zone list.
  • SIDE1: monitor (hear everything, even below squelch). Hold it and turn the knob to set squelch.
  • SIDE2: start or stop a scan. Hold it and turn the knob for big steps.
  • *: mute or unmute the active slot. Press it twice within a second to turn the slot off (chapter 2). Held, the quick menu.
  • #: help: the keys and the menu numbers, a page at a time (# again for the next, BACK to leave). Held, lock or unlock the keys.
  • Digits: type a frequency or channel number. Held, a shortcut (see the key card at the end).

Quick settings

Why the nine things you change most, for the active slot.

  1. On home, press MENU.
  2. Press a digit to jump to that item: 1 squelch, 2 TX power, 3 mode, 4 tones, 5 offset, 6 step, 7 watch, 8 save or edit the channel, 9 Battery Save.
  3. Turn the knob to change it. The change applies at once.
  4. Press BACK to leave. (OK opens a bigger editor for that item.)

0 opens the full menu (More).

The full menu

Why every other setting lives here, in nine groups.

  1. On home, holdMENU. (Or press MENU, then 0.)
  2. Press the group's digit, then the item's digit.

This guide writes menu paths from home in full: MENU052 is APRS messages. On home, holdMENU52 is the same thing with one press less.

In any settings list:

  • ▲▼ move, and the knob changes the value at once.
  • # puts the value back to its default.
  • * undoes the last change for a few seconds.
  • BACK goes back one level.

Edit the slot in place

Why see and change all of a slot's settings on one screen.

  1. On home, holdABC.
  2. Move with the arrows. Turn the knob to change a setting. It applies at once.
  3. Press BACK or ABC when you're done.

The quick menu

Why five switches you might want in the dark, without a menu.

  1. On home, hold*. A small box opens.
  2. Then:
    • knob: screen brightness.
    • PTT A: reverse (listen on a repeater's input).
    • PTT B: night red theme.
    • SIDE1: GPS Off, Auto or On.
    • SIDE2: Bluetooth on or off.
  3. The box closes by itself after 5 seconds, or press BACK.

The PTT keys never transmit while this box is open.

Lock the keys

Why stops pocket presses.

  1. hold#. The keypad locks at once and a padlock shows in the status bar.
  2. For 3 seconds a popup offers more: press * to lock all keys, or 0 for all keys but PTT. Do nothing to keep the keypad lock.
  3. hold# again to unlock, whichever lock it is.
  • Keypad (hold#, let go): the front keys and the knob. PTT, SIDE1 and SIDE2 still work.
  • All but PTT (hold#, then 0): every key, the side keys too. PTT still works.
  • All (hold#, then *): PTT too. The padlock turns red.

The volume knob and hold# always work. The radio always starts unlocked. To lock the keypad by itself after a while: MENU0911.

Volume

Why the first thing you reach for.

  • Turn the knob on top. It is also the power switch.

Squelch

Why squelch keeps the speaker quiet until a signal comes in. Too low and you hear hiss. Too high and you miss weak stations.

  1. Hold SIDE1.
  2. Turn the knob. The level shows large, and you hear the change as you go.
  3. Let go of SIDE1. The level is kept.

Another way: MENU1, then turn the knob. That screen shows a live signal meter, so you can set the level just above the noise.

A channel can have its own squelch: holdABC on the channel and change Squelch.

Ferrite decides squelch itself, from the signal and noise readings. It opens quickly and shuts the moment a station unkeys, so there's no burst of noise at the end. The APRS slot uses the radio chip's own squelch, which holds through a noisy moment so a packet reaches the decoder whole. To use the chip's squelch on every slot, set MENU095 to Chip.

DTMF heard

When someone sends DTMF, such as a radio's ID at key-up or digits dialled on a repeater, the digits show on that slot's card in green: DTMF 101#. They stay a few seconds after the signal ends. Digits only count while the squelch is open, and DTMF decode is off while APRS mode is on. Turn it off with MENU043.

Monitor

Why hear a weak or quiet signal that doesn't open squelch.

  • Hold SIDE1 to listen with squelch open. Let go to stop.
  • Or tap SIDE1 once to keep it open. The slot's card says MON while it stays open. Tap again to close it.

Tune a VFO

Why the VFO tunes any frequency. Use it to explore, or for a frequency you haven't saved.

  1. If the slot shows a channel, press V/M to switch to its VFO.
  2. Turn the knob, or press ▲▼, to step by the slot's step.
  3. SIDE2+knob steps 1 MHz at a time.
  4. ◀▶ change band.

Each slot has its own VFO.

Type a frequency

  1. On a VFO, type the digits. The screen shows the band and whether you may transmit there.
  2. The point goes in by itself after three digits. Six digits enter the frequency: type147330 tunes 147.330 MHz.
  3. For a shorter number, press OK (or *) when you're done: 146*52* tunes 146.520.
  4. BACK deletes a digit.

What you type is tuned exactly.

Step size

  • hold6 moves to the next step size (VFO only).
  • MENU6 picks one from the list.

Channels

Why a channel remembers a frequency with its tones, shift, power and name.

  1. Press V/M to switch the active slot to channels.
  2. Turn the knob, or press ▲▼, to step through the channels in this zone.
  3. SIDE2+knob steps 10 channels.
  4. To go to a channel by number, type it, then press OK.

Zones

Why zones are folders of channels, such as "Repeaters" or "Air". Each channel is in one zone.

  • On a channel, ◀▶ move to the next or previous zone.
  • holdV/M opens the zone list. Move to a zone and press OK. The slot goes to that zone and you're back home.
  • In the zone list, ABC edits the zone (name, scan, priority), # makes a new one, and * deletes one. Deleting a zone keeps its channels.

With a GPS fix, and channels that have a position, a Nearby row at the top lists the channels nearest you.

Browse all channels

Why find, edit, move or delete channels.

  1. MENU021.
  2. Pick a zone, or a view: all channels, by band, recent, or nearby.
  3. Press OK on a channel for its actions: edit, rename, move, renumber, copy to the slot, skip, hide, delete.

Edit a channel

  1. Put the active slot on the channel.
  2. holdABC. The channel's zone, name and frequency are at the top, its settings below.
  3. Move with the arrows and turn the knob to change. Changes apply at once.
  4. BACK when you're done.

Home channel

Why one key back to your favourite channel.

  1. To choose it: holdABC on the channel and set Home to Yes.
  2. To jump there from anywhere on home: hold0.

Copy a channel to the VFO

  1. On the channel, hold9. The radio asks "Copy to VFO?".
  2. Press OK. The slot is now on its VFO, with the channel's settings. The channel is untouched.
  3. Press V/M to go back to the channel.

Mute or turn off a slot

Why silence a busy slot without losing it, or stop listening to it at all.

On home, * works on the active slot:

Press The slot Its card says
* Muted: still receives, but you don't hear it Muted in amber, with RX beside it while a signal is in
* again, more than a second later Back to normal its usual state
** (twice within a second) Off: not received or watched at all a dimmed card with a dashed outline and OFF
* on an off slot Back on its usual state
  • A muted slot still shows activity: its S-meter, RX tag and green LED work. It never takes the speaker from another slot.
  • An off slot's watch turns go to the other slots, so they hear better. It can't transmit, scan or monitor: PTT says "Slot A is off" until you turn it back on.
  • The APRS slot can be muted but not turned off.
  • Mute and off are not saved: turning the radio off and on brings every slot back to normal.

Band scope

Why see activity around your frequency at a glance.

  1. hold1.
  2. ◀▶ move the marker. The knob moves the centre.
  3. # jumps to the strongest signal.
  4. SIDE1 listens to the marker. SIDE2 listens to the strongest.
  5. OK tunes the active slot to the marker.
  6. holdMENU for the scope's settings.

Nothing is heard while the scope sweeps.

Weather radio (US and Canada)

Why NOAA and Environment Canada weather broadcasts, and their alerts.

  • The weather channels come as a zone called Weather. Choose it like any zone.
  • Alerts: MENU045. Turn SAME on and add your location codes. With no codes, every alert shows.
  • An alert fills the screen with a flashing border and a tone. OK silences it. BACK returns to where you were.

Talk

Why the radio's main job.

  1. Hold PTT A and talk, with the radio a few centimetres from your mouth.
  2. Let go to listen.
  • PTT A transmits on the active slot. PTT B always transmits on slot B.
  • The Transmit screen shows the frequency you send on, the power level, your mic level and the time left before the timeout.
  • If the radio won't transmit, the screen says why and which setting fixes it. Press OK to go there.
  • A slot turned off with ** doesn't transmit: press * to turn it back on.

Where you can transmit: your region's amateur bands. You set the region in setup, or at MENU094.

TX power

Why use the least power that works. It saves battery and keeps the channel clear for others.

  • hold2 cycles low, mid and high. A popup shows the new level.
  • Or MENU2, then turn the knob.
  • On 1.25 m the radio always transmits on low: the higher levels give no more power out there, only more heat.

Set up a repeater

Why a repeater relays your signal over a wide area. You need three things from the repeater's listing: its output frequency, its shift (+ or −) and its tone.

The easiest way saves it as a channel:

  1. MENU022 (Add repeater).
  2. Press OK and type the output frequency: type147330. The shift fills in by itself from the band plan.
  3. Press ▼ to Shift. If the listing says otherwise, turn the knob.
  4. Press ▼ to Tone. Turn the knob to the repeater's tone (67.0, 69.3, ... 100.0 ...).
  5. Optional: ▼ to Name, press OK and type a name.
  6. Press OK to save. The channel's edit screen opens so you can check it.
  7. holdBACK to go home. The active slot is on the new channel.

On the VFO instead

Why for a repeater you only need today.

  1. On a VFO, type the output frequency: type147330.
  2. hold7 until the shift you need shows: simplex, +, −.
  3. hold5 opens the tone picker. Type the tone without the point: type885 for 88.5. Press OK.
  4. To keep it: hold9, then OK. It is saved as the next free channel.

The shift amount is 600 kHz on 2 m and 5 MHz on 70 cm. To change it: hold8.

"They can't hear me"

Check these in order:

  • The tone. Most repeaters need one. hold5 shows it.
  • The shift. + or −, from the listing.
  • Reverse is off. The shift shows R when it's on. hold*, then PTT A turns it off.
  • Power. Try hold2 for more.

Tones

Why a tone (CTCSS or DCS) opens a repeater, or keeps other users' traffic quiet on your radio.

  1. hold5 (or MENU4).
  2. RX tone is what you need to hear a station. TX tone is what you send.
  3. ◀▶ choose the kind: Off, CTCSS, DCS-N, DCS-I.
  4. Turn the knob, or type the value (885 is 88.5 Hz).
  5. OK keeps it. * undoes.

Find an unknown tone

Why you hear a repeater's users but don't know its tone.

  1. Tune to the repeater while people are using it.
  2. MENU035. The radio listens and shows the tone it finds.
  3. Press OK to set the RX tone. It then asks about the TX tone: OK sets that too.
  4. BACK cancels and keeps your tones.

When a station is heard but your tone keeps it quiet, the slot's card may show "Heard 88.5". Open MENU, then press OK on Tones to use it.

Tone burst

Some repeaters open with a 1750 Hz burst. Hold PTT and press SIDE2.

Save the VFO as a channel

Why keep a frequency with all its settings.

  1. On the VFO, hold9 (or MENU8).
  2. The form shows the next free number, the zone and the name. Turn the knob to change the highlighted row. OK on Name types one.
  3. OK on any other row saves.

Crossband repeater

Why pass a handheld's signal on to a far repeater, for example from inside a building.

  1. Put one slot on a VHF frequency and another on a UHF frequency, with their tones.
  2. MENU034. Check the two sides and the warnings on the page.
  3. Press # to start.
  4. Any key stops it. Hold SIDE1 to listen in.

While it runs, watch, scan, APRS and Battery Save pause.

Scan

Why let the radio look for activity while you listen.

  1. Press SIDE2. SCN shows in the status bar.
    • On a channel, the radio scans the channels in its zone.
    • On a VFO, it scans the band.
  2. On a signal, the scan stops so you can listen. It goes on by itself a few seconds after the signal ends.
  3. Press SIDE2 again to stop. The slot goes back to where it started.

While scanning:

  • * skips the current signal for the rest of this scan.
  • # skips that channel in every scan from now on.
  • ▲▼ change direction and move on from the current signal.
  • Digits add or remove zones from the scan.
  • OK lists the recent hits.
  • PTT transmits on the frequency the scan stopped on.

Watch: listen to two or three slots

Why keep an ear on a second or third frequency, such as a repeater and a simplex channel, or APRS.

  • hold4 turns watch on or off. A popup shows the new state.
  • MENU7 chooses which slots: off, A+B, or A+B+C.

How it works:

  • The active slot is heard first. A watched slot is heard when the active one is quiet.
  • A watched slot that receives lights up on its card.
  • The radio never changes the active slot by itself. Only you do.
  • To answer on slot B, press PTT B. To answer on slot C, press ABC until C is active, then PTT A.
  • While a watched slot is receiving, # pauses watch for a minute. The bottom bar shows "# Pause".
  • After you transmit, watch waits 30 seconds so you don't miss the reply. The APRS slot keeps listening meanwhile.
  • A slot turned off with ** isn't watched. Its turns go to the other slots.

Watch and scan can run at the same time: one slot can scan while you listen on another.

Frequency counter

Why find the frequency another radio is transmitting on, from close by.

  1. Hold SIDE2 on its own. The counter opens.
  2. Key the other radio close to this one.
  3. The counter shows the frequency and keeps a list of recent hits.
  4. OK copies the frequency to the VFO.

The counter never transmits.

Chapter 5

APRS

APRS is a packet system on one shared frequency (144.390 MHz in North America). Stations send their position, short messages and weather. Ferrite decodes it in the background while you use the radio for voice.

Before you start

  1. Set your callsign: MENU0861. Nothing is sent without one.
  2. Your SSID and map symbol: MENU055. SSID 7 (handheld) is the usual choice. A comment (MENU0553, up to 40 characters) goes out with every beacon. Ambiguity (MENU0556) sends a less exact position. Mic-E (MENU0557) sends shorter beacons with a status: Off duty, En route, In service, Returning, Committed, Special or Priority. Off sends the usual beacon. With no GPS, set a fixed position: MENU0555, and MENU0554 to Fixed to always use it.
  3. The APRS frequency: MENU054. It is set for your region already.

Turn APRS on or off

Why APRS mode gives slot C to APRS. You keep A and B for voice.

  1. MENU053, then knob+1 (or OK).
  2. Slot C tunes to the APRS frequency and decodes in the background. It starts muted: packets are decoded, not heard. * on slot C lets you hear them.
  3. Do the same again to turn it off. Slot C goes back to what it was.

The APRS card

While APRS mode is on, slot C's card shows APRS. The large card (slot C active) shows, from the top:

  • APRS Mode, then the card's state on the right (Muted, RX). When A or B shares C's VHF chip, a note says who comes first: APRS first or A·B first (see below).
  • The frequency, with 3 heard · 11 /hr: the stations heard since you turned the radio on, and in the last hour, so you can tell when the channel is busy.
  • The two newest stations: callsign, how long ago, and their distance and direction from you ("2.4 km NE"). "via digi" means you heard it through a digipeater. With no GPS fix, only the ages show.
  • Unread messages take the second row: "2 new from VE3KPW-9".
  • Your last beacon: "Last beacon digi'd by VE7RHS" when a digipeater repeated it, or "not heard back" in amber.
  • The beacon row: "Beacon in 5m", "Beacon off" or "no fix", with the beacon power at the right.

The small card shows the frequency, the counts and the last station heard.

With slot C active:

  • OK opens the APRS list. OK1 reads your messages.
  • holdV/M sends a beacon now. A tap of V/M only reminds you.
  • PTT A opens a new message. It never sends voice on the APRS frequency.
  • * mutes or unmutes C.

APRS first or A·B first

The radio has one chip for 2 m. When slot A or B is also on that band, the chip takes turns between them and C.

  • APRS first (the default): C gets long turns and A and B short looks. C misses fewer packets; a call on A or B can be heard a moment late.
  • A·B first: A and B get their usual turns, and C misses more packets.

Choose at MENU0597. The note on the card shows only while a slot shares C's chip. With A and B on 70 cm, or turned off, C has the chip to itself and the note goes.

Read messages

Why see what other stations sent you.

An envelope in the status bar means something is unread.

  1. MENU052. Messages are listed newest first: From for received, To for sent, with their state (acked, retry, failed, cancelled).
  2. Move to a message and press OK to read it.
  3. BACK returns to the list.

With APRS mode on and slot C active, you can also press OK on home, then 1.

Reply or send a message

  1. Open the message (above) and press OK to reply.
  2. The quick messages show ("73", "On my way", "Call me" ...). Turn the knob to pick one and press OK to put it in. They're only a shortcut: press * (Write) for an empty message, or just start typing. Turn the knob on an empty message to see them again.
  3. Type your own. Text is predictive: press each letter's key once, turn the knob to pick the word, and * for a space. # goes round T9 (word by word), abc, ABC and 123 (letter by letter); the screen says which.
  4. Punctuation is on 1: press it once for a full stop, again for the next mark: . , ? ! ' / - @ :. The marks show above the hint bar while you pick. hold a digit to type the digit (73, 146.52).
  5. Press OK to send. The message shows "pending" until the other station acknowledges it, and "try 2/5" and so on while the radio sends it again.

The radio tries a message 5 times over about 7 minutes, then marks it failed. Up to 4 messages can be sending at once.

Stop, resend or delete:

  • To stop one that's still sending, select it and holdMENU, then press OK on Stop sending. It stays in the list as "cancelled".

Dynamic clocking

Why the radio spends most of its time waiting for a signal, and it doesn't need full speed to wait.

Ferrite runs the processor at 24 MHz at rest. It goes to full speed (120 MHz) only while something needs it:

  • a key or the knob
  • APRS and the decoders
  • a scan, the band scope and the other special modes
  • transmit
  • a Bluetooth or cable connection

A second after the last need ends, it slows down again. You won't notice the switch.

It's on by default, with nothing to set. On our bench, a resting radio (screen off, receivers sleeping in their cycle) drew about 19 mA instead of about 65 mA at full speed all the time.

APRS mode is the exception: it keeps its receiver awake and the processor at full speed, ready to decode, so it uses noticeably more.

If you ever suspect it, MENU0995 (MCU clock) has Fixed, which keeps full speed always and uses more battery.

Low Power Mode is separate: it turns features off. Dynamic clocking saves power with everything still on.

Install Ferrite on a new radio

Why the first install takes a radio from its stock firmware to Ferrite, with a full backup first.

You need the programming cable, a computer with Chrome, Edge or Firefox (or the desktop app), and about 10 minutes. Ferrite runs on the Radtel RT-950 Pro, and radios built on the same hardware.

  1. Open Firmware › Install and press Start install… (or Install Ferrite on the welcome).
  2. Connect: plug in the cable, turn the radio on, and press Connect cable…. Any firmware is fine.
  3. Back up: press Back up the radio. Console reads the radio's whole memory and checks it, about 4 minutes. Keep the cable in.

Channels and zones

Why the fastest way to set up many channels.

Read and edit:

  1. Open Channels & Zones. Console reads the radio's channels, zones and range presets the first time. Read from radio reads them again.
  2. Edit in the table: name, RX and TX frequencies, tones, power, zone, skip. Shift shows the repeater shift when TX is within 10 MHz of RX ("−600 kHz", "+5 MHz"); type a shift there instead (+600k, -0.6, +5M) and TX follows. A TX further away (crossband, satellites) just shows no shift.
  3. Click a channel's number for the side panel: every field, the options (scan skip, priority, APRS, RX only, hidden, busy lockout, reverse) and Delete channel. On a wide window it sits beside the table; Esc closes it.
  4. + Add channel adds one. All zones and Find narrow the table.

The radio holds 4,000 channels and 512 zones. A zone holds 256 channels at most: a full zone shows (full) in the zone lists, and Console won't put a 257th in it ("Zone 3 Repeaters is full (256 channels at most)"). Moving many at once moves as many as fit and says how many.

  • Position: in the side panel, type degrees (45.4215, -75.6972, north and east positive) or a grid square (FN25dk). The radio uses it for Nearby and distances. A 📍 marks a channel with one.
  • Many at once: tick the rows. The bar above the table sets power, zone, skip or mode for all of them, clears their positions, or deletes them.
  • Undo: ↶ Undo, or Ctrl+Z (⌘Z on a Mac). A delete can also be undone from the notice.

Changed rows are tinted and new ones are green. The sidebar shows "N unwritten" until you write.

Write to the radio

  1. Press Write to radio (N changes).
  2. Console backs the radio's channels up to a file in your downloads. Open that file later to put them back.
  3. It lists every change: added, changed and deleted channels, field by field, and the home channel. Anything the radio would refuse is listed first, to fix.
  4. Press Write N changes. The radio shows the progress; BACK on the radio cancels.
  5. Console reads the channels back and says whether the radio holds the table.

Field notes

How Ferrite works inside the radio, and why it behaves the way it does. You don't need any of this to use the radio. It helps when something surprises you, such as one slot hearing better than another.

Two receivers, two jobs

The radio has two transceiver chips. Each one tunes one frequency at a time.

Chip Covers
Chip 0 64 to 300 MHz: 2 m, 1.25 m, VHF airband and marine
Chip 1 Below 64 MHz, and 300 MHz and up: HF, 6 m, 70 cm, UHF

A third chip handles broadcast FM, AM and shortwave on its own. That's why FM broadcast can play while the radio still watches your slots.

Which chip a slot uses depends only on its frequency. A slot on 146.520 always uses chip 0. A slot on 446.000 always uses chip 1.

One antenna, one way in at a time

Both chips share the antenna. A splitter divides the signal by band, and each band has its own small amplifier in front of its chip. The firmware switches on the amplifier for the band it wants to hear.

The radio is built to have one receive path on at a time. So even with both chips awake, only the chip whose path is on is really listening. This is the main limit behind everything below.

Transmit is different: the crossband repeater listens on one band while it transmits on the other. Each chip does one job, and both paths are on together.

How watch shares time

With watch on, the radio takes turns. Each slot gets the receive path for a moment:

  • The active slot's turn is 150 ms.
  • A watched slot's turn is 70 ms.
  • A slot that hears a signal keeps the path until the signal ends.
  • A slot turned off (**) takes no turns at all.

Two slots on different chips switch fast: each chip stays tuned, and only the path changes. Two slots on the same chip take longer: the chip has to retune and settle before it can hear.

Tip: to watch two frequencies well, put them on different chips, such as one on 2 m and one on 70 cm.

APRS mode

APRS packets are short, about half a second. A packet that starts while the radio listens elsewhere can be missed. So in APRS mode, slot C comes first:

  • When C is alone on its chip (A and B on 70 cm, say), C gets the receive path every other turn, for 100 ms, and the active slot's turn drops to 70 ms. The cycle is A, C, B, C: C hears about 60 % of the time and is never deaf for more than 70 ms.
  • When A or B shares C's chip (both on 2 m), the chip has to retune for each turn. With APRS first (MENU0597, the default) C gets 400 ms turns and the other slots 100 ms looks: C hears about three quarters of the time. A·B first gives A and B their usual turns instead, and C misses more.
  • A slot that hears a signal keeps the path until the signal ends, so a voice call on A still holds C off until it's over.
  • After the radio sends a beacon or message, C keeps the chip for a second, so the digipeater's repeat or the other station's ack is heard from its start. Data transmissions also drop the carrier at once, with no tail.
  • After you talk on A or B, watch pauses for 30 seconds, but C keeps listening.
  • C's chip is set up for data, not voice: two of the filters that shape voice are off on the APRS slot, which helps the decoder.

Tips for the best APRS reception:

  • Put C on a different chip from the slots you watch: A and B on 70 cm.
  • Turn a slot you don't need off (**): its turns go to C.
  • Keep A and B quiet, or turn watch off, when APRS matters most.

What's next: the hardware may allow both receive paths on at the same time. If it does, C could listen all the time while A and B still take turns. We're testing whether reception stays as good with both on before we turn it on.

Squelch: two ways to decide

Each chip can decide squelch by itself. Ferrite can also decide from the chip's signal and noise readings, which opens faster and shuts the moment a station unkeys.

  • Voice slots use Ferrite's squelch.
  • The APRS slot uses the chip's squelch. It holds through a noisy moment, so a packet reaches the decoder whole instead of losing its end.
  • MENU095 set to Chip uses the chip's squelch everywhere.

The decoders listen to the same audio as the speaker. When squelch shuts, they stop hearing too. That's why DTMF digits only count while squelch is open: noise can look like a digit.

Sleep and speed

Most of the time, the radio waits for a signal. Two things save battery while it waits:

  • Battery Save puts the receivers to sleep between short checks (MENU9). Watched slots follow the same cycle.
  • Dynamic clocking runs the processor slowly while nothing needs speed, and at full speed for keys, APRS, transmit and connections.

APRS mode keeps the decoder and C's chip awake, so it uses more battery than plain receive.
